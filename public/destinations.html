↗France

Paris

Paris rewards a neighborhood-led plan: essential monuments, museums and time to experience the city without turning every day into a race.

↗Italy

Rome

Rome works best when ancient sites, the Vatican and its neighborhoods are divided into distinct, walkable days.

↗Greece

Santorini

Santorini is more than an Oia sunset. A balanced route combines caldera villages, beaches, archaeology and unplanned time.

↗Iceland

Iceland

Iceland demands realistic planning around distance, daylight and weather. Reykjavík can be a day-trip base or the start of a road trip.

↗Türkiye

Istanbul

Istanbul makes sense by districts and shores. Sultanahmet, the bazaars, Beyoğlu and the Asian side need a geographic sequence.

↗Brazil

Rio de Janeiro

Rio combines landmark viewpoints, cultural neighborhoods and coastline. Weather and transfers should determine the order of each day.

↗Argentina

Bariloche

Bariloche can combine the city, Circuito Chico and regional days. The season completely changes which routes are realistic.

↗Egypt

Egypt

A first trip to Egypt often connects Cairo, Luxor and Aswan. Transfers and early visits are central to the plan.

↗Norway

Tromsø

Tromsø needs flexible days: northern lights depend on weather and Arctic activities require more time and energy than they appear to.

↗Argentina and Chile

Patagonia

Patagonia is not a single destination. El Calafate, El Chaltén, Torres del Paine and Ushuaia require clear bases and realistic transfers.
